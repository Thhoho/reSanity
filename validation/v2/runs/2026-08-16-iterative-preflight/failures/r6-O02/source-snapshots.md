## E2 - 42 U.S.C. 18795, HOMES

Publisher: U.S. Code / House Office of the Law Revision Counsel

Public state shown by page: text in effect on 2026-07-27

URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&f=treesort&num=1&req=50121

Snapshot content:
- Section 18795 appropriates $4,300,000,000 to DOE for FY2022, available through 2031, for grants to State energy offices to develop and implement a HOMES rebate program.
- State energy offices must submit applications and procedures for modeled/measured savings, certificates, and non-duplication.
- HOMES rebates are for whole-house energy-saving retrofits and have caps tied to savings percentage, project cost, and income category.

Use:
- Text target and statutory budget.

## E3 - 42 U.S.C. 18795a, HEEHR

Publisher: U.S. Code / House Office of the Law Revision Counsel

Public state shown by page: text in effect on 2026-07-19

URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&f=treesort&num=0&req=50122

Snapshot content:
- Section 18795a appropriates $4,275,000,000 for State energy offices and $225,000,000 for Indian Tribes, available through 2031, to develop and implement high-efficiency electric home rebate programs.
- HEEHR is income-restricted and applies to listed electrification projects and enabling upgrades, with per-measure caps and an aggregate household cap.

Use:
- Text target and statutory budget.

## E7 - DOE Program Notice 26-1, HOMES

Publisher: DOE

Effective date: 2026-05-29

URL: https://www.energy.gov/documents/program-notice-26-1

Snapshot content:
- Notice applies to HOMES grantees and tells programs how to proceed whether already launched, not yet launched, or in conditional award status.
- Launched programs must make changes within three months; not-yet-launched grantees must align before launch.
- DOE made changes to consumer choice, stewardship, and grantee implementation, including optional blueprint plans and possible launch after 30 calendar days without written approval.
- DOE recognized multifamily implementation as especially challenging.

Use:
- Shows unresolved administrative/program design frictions before the as-of date.

## E8 - DOE Program Notice 26-2, HEEHR

Publisher: DOE

Effective date: 2026-05-29

URL: https://www.energy.gov/documents/program-notice-26-2

Snapshot content:
- Notice applies to HEEHR state and territory grantees and addresses already launched, not-yet-launched, or conditional award programs.
- Launched programs must conform to the new strategy; not-yet-launched programs must align before launch.
- DOE changed eligibility and implementation rules, including limits on fuel-switching, insulation/air-sealing sequencing before heating/cooling upgrades, shipping/travel cost allowances for territories/AK/HI, and retail/DIY/mail-in pathway flexibility.
- DOE continued to require data/reporting and fraud/waste/abuse controls.

Use:
- Shows that access, sequencing, product availability, contractor/retail pathways, and oversight were still active barriers.

## E13 - DOE OIG Indiana inspection

## E15 - DOE OIG California inspection

Publisher: DOE Office of Inspector General

Public date: 2026-07-21; report date 2026-07-20

URL: https://www.energy.gov/ig/articles/additional-actions-would-strengthen-californias-internal-controls-implementing-home

Snapshot content:
- DOE OIG said California had $581.5M in grants awarded.
- OIG found California had not fully established, documented, and maintained an effective system of internal controls over the federal award.
- Deficiencies included contractor performance monitoring, risk assessment, subcontractor policy/procedure documentation before reservation of funds, and Do Not Pay verification.

Use:
- As-of-admissible evidence that a large award state still had unresolved implementation/oversight barriers.
